iPad, Duo, Watch and TV

Libretto on a bigger screen, on iPhone Duo in every posture, on your wrist and across the room: what each one does, and how to get the most from it.

The short version

  • On iPad, and on an iPhone held sideways, books open as a two-page spread when there’s room, Home spreads into two columns, and the Story view puts the recaps beside the map.
  • On iPhone Duo the page follows the fold: one page closed, two half open like a book, one wide page fully open, and a control deck in tabletop. Folding never loses your place.
  • On Apple Watch, the books you’re reading arrive by themselves. Read a chapter with the Digital Crown, or take a few hours of audiobook out with AirPods and leave your iPhone at home.
  • On Apple TV, read two big pages from the sofa, or press Play/Pause to read along with the audiobook. Recaps and Story Maps come from your iPhone.
  • Your place goes everywhere: whichever you pick up opens where you left off.

What you need

  • iPad: iPadOS 26 or later.
  • iPhone Duo: iOS 26 or later, like any iPhone.
  • Apple Watch: watchOS 11 or later, paired with an iPhone that has Libretto.
  • Apple TV: tvOS 26 or later, signed in to the same Apple Account as your iPhone or iPad, with Sync with iCloud on there (see Sync with iCloud).

iPad

Libretto on iPad is the same app, with the room to spread out.

Reading

  • With Pages set to Automatic (in a book, Aa → Layout), a book is one page held upright and a two-page spread held sideways. Choose One Page or Two Pages to keep one or the other.
  • Any window size works, in Split View or Stage Manager, and your place is kept when the window changes shape.
  • With a keyboard, ← and → turn pages, and space turns to the next page (or plays and pauses while you’re reading along).

Home and the tabs

  • At full width the tabs run across the top, so they name the page themselves.
  • Home is two columns, with Today and Continue running across both.
  • In the Books and Audiobooks tabs, the List view goes into two columns on a wide screen, and a grid’s Cover Size is Large or Small.

Recaps beside the Story Map

Open the Story view in a book (⋯ → Recaps & Story Map) and on a wide screen it’s two panes: the Recaps page in a column on the left, and the mind map filling the rest. They follow each other. Open a chapter or a person on the left and the map shows it; open a profile or a recap from the map and it opens on the left, so the map stays put. The sidebar button at the map’s top left (or ⌃⌘S on a keyboard) folds the Recaps column away to give the map the whole screen, and brings it back. More in Recaps, Story Map and the AI.

Apple Intelligence on iPad

Catch Me Up, Remind Me and Chapter Recaps need an iPad with an M-series or A17 Pro chip, with Apple Intelligence turned on. On other iPads, the recaps and Story Maps your iPhone writes show up through iCloud.

Tip The Finish By widget has an extra-large size on iPad, with the book beside your other books that have a date. See Widgets, Siri and Shortcuts.

iPhone held sideways

  • Books: with Pages on Automatic, two pages side by side when there’s room. If the page controls are on Automatic (Aa → Layout → Page Controls), they move to the right edge, where your thumb rests, and keep clear of the Dynamic Island.
  • Tabs: the title bar steps aside so the page gets the height. The ⋯ button floats in the margin beside the page, with Search first in it.
  • The Story view: on a big iPhone, the recaps and the map side by side, as on iPad.

iPhone Duo

Libretto was designed around iPhone Duo, and the page follows how it’s folded. With Pages on Automatic there’s nothing to set:

Closed
One page on the outside screen, whichever way you hold it and whatever Pages is set to: it’s too small for two. Held upright, the page controls (if they’re on) go down the right, under the camera, never along the bottom.
Half Open, Like a Book
Held sideways and partly open, the inside screen is a two-page spread with the gutter over the fold, like a printed book. With the page controls on the right, each page fills its own side of the fold.
Fully Open
Opened all the way, one wide page with roomy margins, upright or sideways.
Tabletop
Stood up partly open with the fold across, like a tiny laptop: the page stands on the top half, and the bottom half becomes a dark control deck.

Aa → Layout → On iPhone Duo explains each posture in the app, too.

The tabletop deck

In tabletop, the controls move to the flat bottom half: the page buttons, where you are in the book, and your reading stats, with Catch Me Up’s ✨, the bookmark, Blink to Turn Pages’ eye, Auto-Scroll and the ⋯ menu for this book. While you read along, play/pause sits between the page buttons, with the speed close by. The page controls you’d otherwise see on the page stay away in tabletop, since the deck has them.

Glass Deck

Turn on Glass Deck (the deck’s layers button, after the eye, or Aa → Layout → On iPhone Duo; off by default) and, left alone for 3 seconds, the deck’s controls fade away. The bottom half turns to dark glass that reflects the page standing above it: softest by the hinge, blurring out toward you, and moving as the page scrolls, turns or follows the narration. Touch anywhere on the screen to bring the controls back. A tap on the glass only wakes them, so it never presses a button you couldn’t see. Glass Deck stays off while VoiceOver is on, so the controls are always there to find.

Folding keeps your place

Fold, unfold or turn the Duo mid-page and you stay on the same sentence: whatever was at the top of the screen is still there.

Home and the library

Opened up, Home is two columns that meet at the hinge, and the Books grid keeps the fold clear, so no cover sits across it. Held sideways, the tabs sit in a column down the side, under the camera.

Apple Watch

Libretto’s watch app puts the books you’re reading on your wrist, to read with the Digital Crown or listen to with AirPods. It installs with the iPhone app if the Watch app’s automatic install is on; otherwise, open the Watch app on your iPhone and find Libretto under Available Apps.

Your books on the watch

The watch app opens to your books, in two lists:

  • Reading Now: the five books you’ve read or listened to most recently, from Continue on Home. They go to your watch by themselves while Send Books I’m Reading is on.
  • On Your Watch: any other book you’ve sent.

Each row shows the cover, how far you are, and where you read it last if that was another device (“On iPad”). PDFs stay on your iPhone.

Sending a book

In Book Details, find the Apple Watch row and tap Send. A book with a paired audiobook asks how much to take: Text Only, With the Next 2 Hours of Audio (or 4, or 8), or With the Whole Audiobook. For an audiobook on its own, touch and hold it and choose Send to Apple Watch. Change what’s sent from the same place, or take it off with Remove from Apple Watch…: its text and audio come off the watch, and your place stays.

Settings → Apple Watch

This page shows once your iPhone has a watch paired.

It’s about 22 MB an hour, so 4 hours is about 90 MB, for up to three books at a time. It copies while your watch is near your iPhone, and moves on as you listen. Reading with the Digital Crown

Tap a book, then Read. You get a chapter at a time, the whole screen of text in your watch’s own text size; turn the Digital Crown to scroll. Next Chapter waits at the end. The corner button has Contents, Listen from Here and Open on iPhone. The book opens at your newest place on any device (or, for a book with a paired audiobook, where you last listened, if that was later).

Listening with AirPods

Tap Listen; it says how much of the audio is on your watch. It plays to AirPods or other Bluetooth headphones, and if none are connected, the watch offers its headphone picker. You get Back 30 Seconds, Forward 30 Seconds, the speed, the volume on the Digital Crown, and Read, which opens the text where it’s speaking. Your AirPods’ controls and Now Playing work too.

Read Here and Open on iPhone

  • Read Here: with a book open on your iPhone, it’s at the top of the watch’s list (On iPhone), with Read Here under it. Tap it and the book opens on your watch at your iPhone’s place, sent first if it isn’t there yet.
  • Open on iPhone: on a book’s screen, or in the reader, opens the book on your iPhone at the watch’s place. With Libretto on screen there, it happens straight away; otherwise a notification takes you there with a tap. Without notifications, opening Libretto on your iPhone in the next few minutes does it.

Your place, both ways

The watch’s place goes back to your iPhone, and on to your other devices, which open with “Continued from Apple Watch”. Reading and listening on the watch count toward your streak and daily goal, and show in Reading History as “On Apple Watch”. See Sync with iCloud.

Complications

Put Libretto on a watch face or in the Smart Stack: Reading Streak, Today’s Goal (minutes read and listened to today, out of your daily goal) and Currently Reading (tap to read or listen). Each comes in circular, rectangular, inline and corner shapes.

Turning pages from your wrist

With a book open on your iPhone, the watch becomes a page turner: big ◀ ▶ buttons, Double Tap, or a click of the Digital Crown, for reading with your iPhone propped up. Hands-free reading has all of it, including Listen on Apple Watch.

Tip To keep Libretto on screen while you read on the watch, open the watch’s Settings app → General → Return to Clock → Libretto, and choose After 1 Hour.

Good to know Books and audio reach your watch from your iPhone, so they copy while the two are near each other, and hours of audio can take a while. Once they’re on the watch, reading and listening don’t need your iPhone.

Apple TV

Libretto on Apple TV is your library from across the room: big pages to read from the sofa, or the audiobook playing with the text following along. Each person on the Apple TV has their own library, with their own Apple Account.

How your books get there

Apple TV can’t open ebooks itself, so once your TV has opened Libretto, your iPhone or iPad makes each book’s text for it and puts it in iCloud, by itself. Your place, recaps and reading time go both ways. The text on the TV is plain: no pictures or italics, and your highlights stay on your iPhone and iPad. PDFs stay there too.

What it shows

Home has the book you’re on, big, with its buttons, and shelves under it: Continue Reading, Read Along, Audiobooks, Recaps and Story Maps, Recently Added and Finished. The big book follows you as you move along a shelf.

Reading

The reader sets the book as two pages side by side, or one column, in a serif face sized for across the room, with Night, Dusk, Sepia or Paper pages. Click or swipe right and left to turn the page, press Select for the menu, and Back to leave. The menu has Contents, Recaps, Story Map, Auto-Scroll, Read Along, the text size, the page color, and One Page or Two Pages. The book opens at your newest place on any device (“Continued from iPhone”).

Read-Along and Auto-Scroll

Press Play/Pause and the page starts moving:

  • Read-Along, when the book’s audiobook is on the TV: the word being read is marked and the pages turn with it. Right and left skip 30 seconds on and 15 back.
  • Auto-Scroll otherwise: one column moving up at a steady pace. Up and down change its speed, in words a minute.

Recaps and the Story Map

Chapter Recaps, The Story So Far, the Story Map, and People and Places show what your iPhone wrote, for the chapters you’ve read on any device. On the map, every name is a stop for the remote: Select folds it or opens it, and Play/Pause opens its page. Apple TV doesn’t write recaps itself.

Getting an audiobook from your iPhone

  1. On the TV, open the book’s page and choose Get Audiobook from iPhone.

  2. Your iPhone gets a notification. Tap it (or just have Libretto open there) and it makes a smaller copy for the TV, starting where you are.

  3. The copy goes up to iCloud in 30-minute parts, and the TV can start playing as soon as the first part is there.

The copy is about 29 MB an hour, and it stays in iCloud until you remove it: on the TV with Remove from Apple TV… on its page, or on your iPhone in Settings → Apple TV, Book Details’ Apple TV row, or the audiobook’s touch-and-hold menu. Each asks first. You can send one from your iPhone without asking on the TV, too: Book Details’ Apple TV row → Send, or touch and hold the audiobook → Send to Apple TV. To get the TV’s requests, allow notifications in Settings → Apple TV on your iPhone. That page shows once a TV has synced.

Top Shelf

With Libretto in the top row of the Apple TV Home Screen, the Top Shelf shows Continue Reading. Select a book for its page, or press Play to open it.

Questions

Does my watch need my iPhone nearby?

To get books and audio, yes: they come from your iPhone. Once they’re there, you can read and listen without it, and your place goes back the next time the two are together. The page turner and Open on iPhone need the iPhone in reach.

Why aren’t my PDFs on my watch or TV?

Libretto sends ebooks and audiobooks to the watch and the TV. PDFs keep their own fixed pages, and they stay on your iPhone and iPad.

My Duo shows one page when it’s half open. Why?

Check Aa → Layout: Pages should be Automatic or Two Pages, and the Duo held sideways. With One Page, it’s always one page.

Can I read on Apple TV without an iPhone?

Your iPhone or iPad prepares the books for the TV, so it needs one with Libretto and Sync with iCloud on. After that, you can read on the TV whenever you like, wherever your iPhone is.

My iPad doesn’t have Apple Intelligence. Can it still show recaps?

Yes. With Sync with iCloud on, the recaps and Story Maps your iPhone writes show on the iPad, for the same book. It just can’t write its own.

How much room does audio take on my watch?

About 22 MB an hour. The default, the next 4 hours, is about 90 MB a book, for up to three books. Libretto lets go of audio well behind your place as you listen.

Can several people use Libretto on one Apple TV?

Yes. Each person on the Apple TV has their own library, from their own Apple Account.
