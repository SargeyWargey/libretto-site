Getting started

Your first books in, your way around the tabs, and the one page that knows everything about a book. Start here if you’ve just installed Libretto, or if you’d like to know what every corner of the library does.

The short version

  • Add books with ⋯ → Add to Library: Choose Files…, Download from Link… or Free Classics. Or share a file to Libretto from Files or any other app.
  • Ebooks and audiobooks sort themselves. Libretto opens DRM-free EPUB, PDF, comics, FB2, text, Markdown, HTML and RTF, and audiobooks in M4B, MP3 and other common formats.
  • The first time you open a book, choose Start Reading to keep your place and reading time, or Just Browse to look around without saving anything.
  • Home gathers what you’re reading (Continue), your goals and your library. Customize Home, at the bottom of Home, picks its sections.
  • Long-press any cover for the book’s menu. Book Details, at the top of it, is the one page with everything about that book.
  • Stuck? Settings → About → Tips & Guide explains each big feature, with a Show Me button that takes you there.

Your first launch

The first time you open Libretto, its icon wakes up in a short animation (tap to skip it). Then four cards say what Libretto does:

Read
Any EPUB or PDF, with real pages in the font and theme you like, and two pages side by side on an iPhone Duo.
Listen Together
Pair a book with its audiobook and the words light up as they’re read.
Remembers the Story
Catch-up recaps and a map of who’s who, written on your iPhone from the pages you’ve read. Recaps need Apple Intelligence.
Bring Your Shelves
Connect Hardcover and your Want to Read, Currently Reading and Read shelves come along, and Libretto keeps your progress there up to date. Tap Connect Hardcover, or Not Now.

Skip on the first three cards jumps to the Hardcover one. Hardcover is free at hardcover.app, and you can connect it any time in Settings → Hardcover. To see the cards again, open Settings → About → Welcome Tour.

With nothing in your library yet, Home shows a Welcome to Libretto card with Choose Files and Free Classics, the two quickest ways to a first book. The Books tab has the same two buttons. Until Hardcover is connected, Home also has a Bring Your Hardcover Shelves card, with Connect Hardcover and Not Now to put it away.

Tip Free Classics is the fastest way to try everything: pick a public-domain book you know, like Pride and Prejudice, and you’ll be reading in seconds.

Getting books in

Home, Books and Audiobooks each have a ⋯ button at the top. Its Add to Library menu has three ways in, and you can also send files to Libretto from other apps.

From the Files app

⋯ → Add to Library → Choose Files… opens the Files picker. Pick as many files as you like, ebooks and audiobooks together, from anywhere Files can reach: iCloud Drive, On My iPhone, or a cloud service you’ve added there. Each one goes where it belongs, and a notice says what came in (“Added 1 book and 1 audiobook.”).

From a link

⋯ → Add to Library → Download from Link… asks for a web address. Paste a link to a DRM-free book (EPUB, PDF and others), an audiobook file, or a zip of either, and tap Download.

Free Classics

⋯ → Add to Library → Free Classics (on Home and Books) opens Popular Classics, free public-domain books from Project Gutenberg. Search by title or author, and tap the download button beside a book to add it.

From other apps

In Files, long-press a book, an audio file or a zip, tap Share and choose Libretto (or tap the file and pick Libretto). The same works from Mail, Messages and any app with a Share button. It’s added whichever tab you’re on: a single book opens in the reader and a single audiobook in the player, and several files shared together are added together.

Libretto’s folder in Files

Libretto has a folder of its own in the Files app, On My iPhone → Libretto (On My iPad on an iPad). Drop files there and they’re added the next time you come back to Libretto.

How files sort themselves

You never have to say whether something is a book or an audiobook. Libretto looks at what arrives:

  • A book file becomes a book.
  • Audio files added together become one audiobook, in file-name order. An M4B with chapter markers keeps its chapters; otherwise each file is a chapter.
  • A zip of audio becomes an audiobook. An EPUB or FB2 beside the tracks is added as a book too; a PDF or read-me there is left out.
  • A zip of books adds each one (EPUB, PDF, FB2 and CBZ; text files only when there’s nothing else in it).
  • A zip that’s really an EPUB, or holds nothing but a comic’s pages, is read as one book.

When you add an ebook or an audiobook that matches an unpaired one of the other kind, Libretto asks Read and Listen Together? Tap Pair Them and the words light up as the narrator reads them (see Read-Along and audiobooks), or Not Now to leave them for later.

Good to know A book that’s already in your library (the same title, author and format) isn’t added twice. Libretto also checks there’s room first, and says how much space a file needs if your device is too full.

What Libretto can open

Ebooks

  • EPUB: the standard ebook format, and the one Libretto does the most with.
  • PDF: its own pages, one at a time or two side by side.
  • Comics (CBZ): a page per screen, or two in a spread.
  • FB2 (FictionBook): its sections, poems, pictures and cover.
  • Plain text: chapters are found from headings like “Chapter”, “Part” and “Book”, and Project Gutenberg’s header and footer are taken off.
  • Markdown: headings become chapters.
  • HTML and RTF: read as one document.

Everything but PDF is turned into the same kind of pages when it’s added, so one reader handles them all. Book Details still says what the file was.

Audiobooks

M4B, M4A, MP3, AAC, FLAC, WAV, AIFF and CAF, as single files, several files together, or a zip of them.

Good to know Libretto opens DRM-free files only. Books and audiobooks protected with DRM by another store can only be opened in that store’s app. Kindle’s own formats (AZW, AZW3 and MOBI) and CBR comics aren’t supported.

Start Reading or Just Browse

The first time you open a book you haven’t started, Libretto asks Start Reading? It’s there so that flicking through a book to see what it’s like doesn’t fill your stats, move it into Continue or tell Hardcover you’ve begun.

Start Reading
Libretto keeps your place and reading time in the book, counts it toward your goals and stats, and updates Hardcover if the book is linked there. With Settings → Goals → Ask for a Finish Date on (it is to start with), Libretto then offers to set a day to finish it by: see Finish By dates.
Just Browse
Nothing is saved: not your place, reading time, stats or Hardcover progress. The menu says “Browsing: your progress isn’t saved”, with a Start Reading button if you change your mind.

If you close a book you were browsing after reading on, Libretto asks Save Your Progress? Save Progress keeps it all, from the moment you opened the book. Don’t Save leaves the book as it was, and it asks again next time.

A book whose paired audiobook you’ve been listening to already counts as started, so it doesn’t ask.

The tabs

Libretto opens on Home. These are the tabs it can show, and whether each is on to start with:

Home Always
One page of sections: today’s goal and streak, what you’re reading, your library and more. See Home.
Books On
Your ebooks as a grid or a list. The chips under the title sort and filter them: Sort, Reading, Not Started, Finished, Read-Along, and with Sync with iCloud on, On This iPhone and Elsewhere.
Audiobooks On
The same for audiobooks. Tap one to play it.
Shelf With Hardcover
Your Hardcover shelves, for finding your next book. It shows once Hardcover is connected: see Hardcover.
Read-Along Off
Your book-and-audiobook pairs, and the ones ready to pair. See Read-Along and audiobooks.
Notebook Off
Every highlight, note and bookmark from all your books in one place, with search, for the quote you remember but not which book it was in.
Reading Stats Off
Your goals, the last seven days, and your year. See Reading Stats.

Choose them in Settings → Home & Tabs, under Home’s sections, and tap Edit to put them in order. Home is always first, with room for up to four more; with five showing, turn one off to make room for another. Restore Default Tabs puts them back as they came.

The ones that aren’t tabs are still close: Home’s sections open them with See All, and when one is opened that way, its ⋯ menu has Show as a Tab.

The ⋯ menu

Each tab has one ⋯ button at the top, holding that tab’s actions: Add to Library on Home, Books and Audiobooks; Select, Sort By, Filter, View As and Cover Size on Books and Audiobooks; the Notebook on Home and Books. Settings is always the last item. On an iPhone held sideways, the ⋯ button floats beside the page, with Search first in it.

Home

Home is one page of sections, Today first. A section with nothing to show stays hidden, so a new library starts simple and fills in as you read.

Today
A ring of today’s reading and listening minutes toward your Daily Reading Goal, your streak, and the next thing to read in plain words (“18 pages tonight keeps you on track with Emma”). See Today’s Target Bar and Home’s Today.
Continue
Every book and audiobook you’re partway through. See below.
Books and Audiobooks
A row of covers. In the section’s options, show them all by Recently Opened, Recently Added or Title, or only the Not Started or Finished ones. While Continue is on, they leave out what’s already there.
Read-Along
Your pairs, and how many more are ready to pair.
Shelf
With Hardcover, a shelf seen through your own filters, like Want to Read or “under 200 pages”. Add as many as you like with Add Shelf Section.
Reading Stats
Tiles you choose and order: your yearly goal, Finish By dates, pages, time, streak, and Hardcover’s numbers. Tap one for that part of Reading Stats.
Notebook
The books you’ve highlighted, noted or bookmarked, with the counts on each cover. A cover opens that book’s notes; See All opens the Notebook.

Customize Home

Tap Customize Home at the bottom of Home (it’s also Settings → Home & Tabs). Turn sections on and off, tap one with › for its options, or tap Edit to drag them into order. Restore Default Layout puts everything back, after asking.

You can also long-press a section’s title: Edit Section… (or Customize Home…) and Hide Section…, which asks first. Hiding a section never removes anything in it.

See All on a section opens its tab, or its own page when it isn’t a tab. On an iPad, or an iPhone Duo opened up, Home spreads into two columns.

Continue

Continue holds every ebook and audiobook you’re partway through, most recent first. With Sync with iCloud on, reading on your other devices counts too, so a book you’ve been reading on your iPad shows here too. Each card takes its color from its cover.

  • Tap a card to go straight back in, at your place.
  • A book paired with its audiobook is one card with both covers, and Read and Listen along its foot. Read opens the book; Listen plays the audiobook from your place, in Now Playing. Handy when you’re about to go for a walk.
  • Long-press a card and choose Remove from Continue to take it off (a pair goes as one) until you open or play it again. Putting a book on the Paused or Did Not Finish shelf in Book Details does the same.
  • For a book with a Finish By date, a thin line along the bottom of its card fills toward tonight’s pages.

A book’s long-press menu

Long-press a book’s cover anywhere (Home, the Books tab, search results) for the things you do most:

  • Book Details, first, with “Rating, progress, pairing and more” under it.
  • Read, Continue Reading, or Open once it’s finished; Read Along for a book paired with its audiobook.
  • Highlights, with how many, when it has some: straight to its list.
  • Mark as Read or Mark as Unread.
  • Share Card…, an image to share: see Share Cards.
  • Remove…, which asks first and says what goes with it.

Everything else, from pairing to Reset Progress, is in Book Details. An audiobook’s menu has its own set: Pair with Ebook…, Mark as Finished, Edit Details…, Listening History and the rest (see Read-Along and audiobooks).

Book Details

Book Details is the one page for every book. Open it from a book’s long-press menu, from ⋯ → Book Details while you’re reading, or from a book on your Hardcover shelves. What you open from it slides in on top, and ✕ closes it.

The top

A large cover on a wash of its color, the title, author and series (Hardcover’s, or the book file’s own, like “Wayward Children, Book 2”), and Hardcover readers’ rating. Under them, a line in words: “Reading · p. 96 of 448 · 21% · started Sep 14”, “Not started · Added Oct 8”, or “Finished Oct 2”.

Then the main button (Read Along, Continue Reading, Read or Open Book) and three chips:

Rate
Your star rating: Hardcover’s, for a book linked there, or otherwise Libretto’s own, which goes to your other devices with the book.
Shelf
Hardcover’s shelves for a linked book, or Libretto’s own: Want to Read, Reading, Finished, Paused and Did Not Finish. Paused and Did Not Finish take the book out of Continue until you open it again.
Mark as Read
Or Mark as Unread. See Mark as Read.

Your Reading

A progress bar (“45% read · p. 224 of 477”), with tonight’s mark on it if the book has a Finish By date; the Finish By line with Change, or Set a Finish Date…; Reading History, every session with the book (“14 sessions · 6 h 20 min”); and Notebook, its highlights, notes and bookmarks.

Story

Once chapter recaps have been written: the start of The Story So Far, the five people mentioned most, and Recaps and Story Map. See Recaps, Story Map and the AI.

Listening

For EPUBs: its paired audiobook, with Listen and Unpair…, or Pair with Audiobook…; an Apple Watch row when the watch app is installed, and an Apple TV row once you have a TV with Libretto and a paired audiobook. See Read-Along and audiobooks and iPad, Duo, Watch and TV.

About

The description (Hardcover’s, or the book’s own), its genres and moods, four facts (Hardcover’s readers, how many have read it, pages and year published; or, for a book that isn’t on Hardcover, its format, size, language and the day you added it), View on Hardcover, and Similar Books.

The buttons at the top

Share Card…, and a ⋯ menu with the less everyday things: Edit Details…, Change Cover… or Restore Original Cover, Hardcover (the edition, Change Match… and Send Progress Now, or Match on Hardcover… for a book that isn’t linked), Reset Progress… and Remove from Library….

Searching your library

Pull down at the top of Books (or scroll back up) for Search Books; Audiobooks has the same. They look through titles and authors. Home’s search field looks through your ebooks, audiobooks and, with Hardcover, the books on your shelves.

Every word you type has to appear, in any order, and case and accents don’t matter: “bronte jane” finds Jane Eyre.

To search the words inside a book, use Search Book in the reader (see Reading); to search every highlight and note you’ve made, use the Notebook.

Edit Details and Change Cover

Files from elsewhere sometimes come with odd titles (“Moby Dick (z-lib.org) [Retail]”) or authors backwards (“Melville, Herman”). Fix them in Book Details → ⋯ → Edit Details… (for an audiobook, long-press it → Edit Details…). The library, the reader, Home, the widgets, the Lock Screen and Hardcover matching all use what you type. Use the File’s Title and Author puts the originals back.

For a book matched on Hardcover, Book Details → ⋯ → Change Cover… offers the covers Hardcover has for it, its own and its editions’. Restore Original Cover goes back to the one in the file.

Tip If a book won’t match on Hardcover, fix its title and author with Edit Details first, then try Match on Hardcover… again.

Mark as Read

Mark as Read (in the long-press menu, Book Details, or ⋯ while reading) finishes a book at once, however far you’ve got: it gets the Read ribbon, leaves Continue, counts toward your Yearly Book Goal and shows as finished on Home. Mark as Unread undoes it.

For a book linked to Hardcover, it updates Hardcover too: Read closes the read-through today and moves the book to your Read shelf; Unread reopens it there as Currently Reading. A notice says what happened, or why Hardcover couldn’t be changed.

Reading on in the book undoes the mark, and Libretto goes by how far you’ve actually read again.

In Books or Audiobooks, ⋯ → Select lets you pick several covers and mark them all at once.

Reset Progress

Book Details → ⋯ → Reset Progress… (an audiobook: its long-press menu) puts a book back at the beginning, as if you’d never started it, so opening it asks Start Reading? again. A book and its paired audiobook share one place, so both go back.

It asks first. Your highlights, bookmarks and reading stats stay, and so does your shelf and progress on Hardcover.

Removing a book

Remove… in the long-press menu (or Book Details → ⋯ → Remove from Library…) takes a book off this device. It asks first and says exactly what goes with it: its highlights and notes, reading sessions, recaps and Story Map, and any Finish By date or Hardcover link.

  • Its bookmarks are kept, in case it comes back.
  • With Sync with iCloud on, its highlights and notes stay in iCloud too, and return if you add the same book again. Your other devices keep their own copies (see Sync with iCloud).
  • A paired audiobook stays in your library, unpaired.

Tips & Guide, and where to go next

Libretto shows small tips where they help, once each, the first time a feature is useful: tapping the line under the page, the ⋯ menu in a book, Customize Home and others.

For the whole picture, open Settings → About → Tips & Guide. It has a paragraph on each of Read, Listen Together, Hands-Free, The Story, Goals, and Watch and TV, each with Show Me to go straight there. Show Tips Again at the bottom brings back the tips you’ve already seen.

Then pick what you’d like to do next:

  • Reading: the page, the menus, themes and fonts, highlights and search.
  • Read-Along and audiobooks: pairing a book with its audiobook so the words light up as they’re read.
  • Hands-free reading: Auto-Scroll and Blink to Turn Pages.
  • Recaps, Story Map and the AI: Catch Me Up, Remind Me and chapter recaps.
  • Sync with iCloud: your library and place on every device.
  • Goals, stats and sharing: streaks, finish dates and share cards.
  • Hardcover: your shelves and progress.
  • Every setting: what each option does.

Questions

Can I read books I bought on Kindle, Apple Books or Audible?

Only if they’re DRM-free. Books and audiobooks protected with DRM by another store can only be opened in that store’s app. Public-domain classics (try Free Classics) and books from stores and publishers that sell DRM-free files work perfectly.

My audiobook came as lots of MP3 files. How do I add it?

Pick them all at once in Choose Files… (or share them together, or add a zip of them). Files added together become one audiobook, in file-name order, with each file as a chapter.

I chose Just Browse by mistake. Have I lost my place?

Not if you act before closing the book: tap the middle of the page for the menu and tap Start Reading. If you close it, Libretto asks Save Your Progress?; choose Save Progress to keep everything from when you opened it.

A book I’m not reading any more keeps showing in Continue.

Long-press its card and choose Remove from Continue, or put it on the Paused or Did Not Finish shelf in Book Details. It comes back if you open it again.

Where are Reading Stats, the Notebook and Read-Along?

They’re on Home (each section’s See All), and you can make any of them a tab: Settings → Home & Tabs, or Show as a Tab in the page’s own ⋯ menu.

Why isn’t there a Shelf tab?

Shelf shows your Hardcover shelves, so it appears once Hardcover is connected in Settings → Hardcover. See Hardcover.

Does removing a book delete it from my other devices?

No. Remove… takes it off the device you’re holding. Your other devices keep their copies; see Sync with iCloud for how books on other devices show up.

Why does the opening animation play only sometimes?

It plays when Libretto starts afresh: the first time, and after you close it from the App Switcher. Coming back to Libretto from another app goes straight to where you were. Tap the animation to skip it.
