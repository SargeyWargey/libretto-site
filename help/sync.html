Sync with iCloud

How Libretto keeps your library, your place and your reading the same on your iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch and Apple TV: what travels through your iCloud, what stays on each device, and what to do when something hasn’t arrived.

The short version

  • Libretto syncs through your own iCloud. There’s no Libretto account and no Libretto server: sign in to the same Apple Account on each device and that’s it.
  • It’s on from the start. You’ll find it in Settings → iCloud & Storage.
  • Your whole library comes along: every book’s details and cover, your place, highlights, notes and bookmarks, recaps and Story Maps, Read-Along pairs, your reading log, goals and streak, and your Hardcover sign-in.
  • Open a book anywhere and it’s at the newest page you reached on any device. A “Continued from iPad” pill takes you back if you’d rather not.
  • With Store Books in iCloud on, your other devices can download the books themselves: under 100 MB by themselves, bigger ones (most audiobooks) with a tap.
  • Settings like page themes and fonts stay with each device.

How sync works

Libretto keeps a copy of your library’s details in your own iCloud, in a private space that belongs to your Apple Account. Each of your devices sends what changed on it and picks up what changed on the others. There’s nothing to sign up for, and nobody else can see it, Libretto’s developer included.

All it needs:

  • Every device signed in to the same Apple Account, with iCloud turned on for Libretto (in the Settings app, tap your name, then iCloud).
  • Sync with iCloud on in Libretto on each of them. It’s on unless you’ve turned it off.
  • For the book files themselves, iCloud Drive turned on as well (see Books on your other devices).

Libretto recognizes a book by what’s inside the file, not by its name. The same EPUB added on your iPhone and on your iPad is one book, wherever each copy came from, so its place, highlights and recaps line up. A different file of the same title (another edition, or a copy from another store) is a different book with its own place, because its words don’t line up page for page.

Apple Watch works a little differently: it talks to your iPhone, and your iPhone passes its place and reading time on. There’s more in Apple Watch and Apple TV.

Turning it on, and seeing how it’s going

Open Settings (the last item in any tab’s ⋯ menu) and tap iCloud & Storage. Sync with iCloud is at the top, and it’s on unless you’ve turned it off. Under it, a line says how it’s going:

  • Up to date, or “Up to date · 2 min ago”: everything’s been sent and fetched.
  • Syncing…: it’s working on it.
  • Waiting for a connection: you’re offline. It carries on when you’re back.
  • iCloud storage is full. Libretto will try again.: make room in iCloud (or get more), and it picks up from there.
  • Sign in to iCloud in the Settings app, and turn on iCloud for Libretto.: this device isn’t signed in, or iCloud is off for Libretto.
  • iCloud is restricted on this device.: something like Screen Time or a work profile is stopping it.

Settings’ first page shows the short version beside iCloud & Storage: Sync On, Sync Off, Not signed in to iCloud, iCloud storage is full, or Couldn’t sync.

Every option on iCloud & Storage

Sync with iCloud On
Keeps this device in step with your others. Off, this device keeps everything it has and stops sending and fetching (see Turning sync off).
Store Books in iCloud On
Keeps the files of the books and audiobooks you add in Libretto’s storage in your iCloud Drive, so your other devices can download them. Each book goes up once, when you add it or when you turn this on. Off, books you add stay on this device, and books already in iCloud stay there.
Download Larger Books Automatically Off
Books under 100 MB download to your other devices by themselves. Larger ones, like most audiobooks, wait for a tap unless this is on.
Other Devices
Each of your other devices that has synced, with its days of reading, finished books, books, and when it last synced, and a Remove button (below).
Storage
What Libretto keeps on this device, and clearing what can be downloaded or made again.

The two Books in iCloud switches show while Sync with iCloud is on. They need iCloud Drive turned on for Libretto in the Settings app, and until it is, the page says so. Their footer also says how much room your books take in iCloud.

Tip Once a book is safely in iCloud, Libretto lets go of iCloud’s own extra copy on that device, so a book never takes its space twice.

Removing another device’s reading

Sold an iPad, or signed a borrowed device in to your account for a while? Under Other Devices, tap Remove beside it, then Remove from iCloud. Its reading days, sessions, finished books and places in books are deleted from iCloud and from all your devices, so they stop counting in your stats and streak. Your books, highlights and notes stay. If that device syncs again later, what it reads from then on comes back.

What syncs

  • Your library: every book’s and audiobook’s title, author and cover, including a title you fixed with Edit Details and a cover you chose with Change Cover. Books that are only on another device still show (see Books on your other devices).
  • Your place and progress in every ebook, PDF and audiobook (see Your place on every device).
  • Highlights, notes and bookmarks. Change a note on one device and it changes on the others; delete a highlight and it goes everywhere. If two devices change the same highlight before they hear from each other, the newest change wins.
  • Book details: Mark as Read, the day you started, Remove from Continue, and Libretto’s own rating and shelf for books that aren’t on Hardcover.
  • Chapter Recaps and Story Maps, and which chapters you’ve read on each device, so recaps stay spoiler-free everywhere. A chapter recapped on one device isn’t written again on another, and an iPhone or iPad without Apple Intelligence shows the recaps your other devices wrote. More in Recaps, Story Map and the AI.
  • Read-Along pairs, once they’re prepared. Pair a book with its audiobook on one device, and every device that has both is ready to read along, with nothing to prepare again.
  • Your reading log: reading and listening days and sessions, with the device each was on (Reading History says “On iPad”), and the books you’ve finished. Pages and minutes from every device add up, so your streak and stats are the same on all of them.
  • Your goals: the Daily Reading Goal, Daily Page Goal, Yearly Book Goal and each book’s Finish By date. If two devices change different goals before they hear from each other, both changes stick. More in Goals, stats and sharing.
  • Hardcover: your sign-in, through iCloud Keychain, and which Hardcover book and edition each of your books is, so a match you confirm on one device isn’t asked about on another. More in Hardcover.
  • Book files, while Store Books in iCloud is on.

What stays on each device

Some things are better set per device, so they don’t sync:

  • Appearance: page theme, font, text size, layout, page controls and page turns, plus the app theme and app icon. Your iPad can have Night pages while your iPhone stays on Sepia.
  • Listening and hands-free settings, like playback speed, the Read-Along marker, Auto-Scroll and Blink to Turn Pages.
  • Home and the tabs: Home’s sections, which tabs you show, and how each library tab is sorted, filtered and laid out.
  • The Streak Reminder, which each device schedules for itself.
  • Catch Me Up and Remind Me, which are written fresh on the device you ask on.
  • Book files, unless Store Books in iCloud is on.

Your place on every device

Libretto saves your place as the words at the top of the screen, not as a percentage. So another device opens on the page with those same words, whatever its screen size, font or layout.

The newest place wins

Open a book and Libretto goes to the newest place you reached in it, on any device. If that isn’t where this device left off, a glass pill floats at the bottom of the page, the same kind as Back to Narration:

“Continued from iPhone · Tap to go back to Chapter 9 · 23 pages back”

  • Tap the pill to go back to where you were on this device. That becomes your newest place.
  • Tap ✕ to keep the new place and put the pill away. Or just read on: after six pages it goes by itself.

It’s the newest place that wins, not the furthest. If you went back to reread a chapter, jumped to the notes at the end, or used Reset Progress, that’s where your other devices open next.

It never moves the page while you’re reading

Libretto only moves you when you open the book, when you come back to Libretto with the book open, or when you’ve left it alone for a couple of minutes. A newer place that arrives while you’re turning pages waits its turn. So two devices open side by side don’t tug the page back and forth.

Books you haven’t opened on this device

A book you started somewhere else opens straight at your newest place, with no pill and no “Start Reading?” question.

PDFs and audiobooks

PDFs get the same pill, by page. Audiobooks get it in Now Playing, above the controls, when another device is more than 30 seconds away. Now Playing doesn’t jump while it’s playing.

Progress everywhere is the newest

The percentage on Home, in Continue, in the Books and Audiobooks tabs, in Book Details, in the widgets and in Shortcuts is your newest anywhere, so every device shows the same number. A book you read on another device moves to the front of Continue, and books you’re reading only on another device are listed there too, with where they are (“38% · On iPad”). Remove from Continue works on every device at once.

Books on your other devices

With sync on, the Books and Audiobooks tabs show your whole library, not just what’s on this device. A book that isn’t here shows a little dimmed, with a badge in its corner: the symbol of the device it’s on, or a cloud if it’s in iCloud. Under it, a line says where it is (“On iPad”, “In iCloud · 12 MB”) or how far you are.

Show just what’s here, or just what isn’t

While sync is on, the chip row under the tab’s title (and Filter in its ⋯ menu) has On This iPhone (On This iPad on an iPad) and Elsewhere. Tap one to show only those books, and tap it again to show everything.

Downloading a book

Tap a book that’s in iCloud to download it. A ring on its badge fills as it comes. Books under 100 MB don’t need the tap: they download by themselves as soon as they reach iCloud. Bigger ones, like most audiobooks, wait for you, unless Download Larger Books Automatically is on.

When a book arrives, everything else comes with it: your edited title, Mark as Read, highlights, notes, bookmarks and reading history. It opens at your newest place.

Touch and hold a book that isn’t here for Book Details: how far you are and where you read it last, each device that has it, and, if it’s in iCloud, a Download button with its size.

A book that’s only on another device

If a book isn’t in iCloud (Store Books in iCloud was off on the device that has it, say), this device can’t download it. Turn on Store Books in iCloud on the device that has it, or add the same file here from Files or AirDrop. Libretto recognizes it and brings your place, highlights and notes along.

Good to know A book you removed from this device doesn’t download again by itself. Tap it when you want it back.

Removing a book: from here, or from your library

A book can go in three ways. Libretto always asks first, and says what goes.

Remove… (a book on this device)
Touch and hold it and choose Remove…, or in Book Details, ⋯ → Remove from Library…. It leaves this device only: its file, reading history, Read-Along pairing, Story Map, Finish By date and Hardcover link go from here. Your other devices keep their copies. Its highlights and bookmarks wait in iCloud and come back if you add the same file again. If another device or iCloud still has the book, it stays in your library here, as a book that’s elsewhere.
Remove… (a book that isn’t on this device)
It leaves the library on every device that doesn’t have it, and its copy in iCloud is deleted. Devices that have it keep it. Add the file again any time to bring it back, with your place, highlights and notes.
Delete from iCloud… (a book that’s in iCloud but not here)
Deletes only the copy in iCloud. Devices that have the book keep it, and others can’t download it any more. Your place, highlights and notes stay.

To remove a book from every device

  1. On each device that has the book’s file, touch and hold it and choose Remove….

  2. The book now shows under Elsewhere. On any device, touch and hold it and choose Remove… again. That takes it out of your library everywhere and deletes its copy in iCloud.

Good to know Removing a book doesn’t take it out of your stats: you still read it. To take a book out of your finished count, touch and hold it in Reading Stats → In Libretto → Finished and choose Remove from Finished. That goes for every device.

Apple Watch and Apple TV

Apple Watch

The watch doesn’t use iCloud itself: it talks to your iPhone. Your place on the watch goes to your iPhone, which passes it to iCloud as the watch’s own, so your iPad opens with “Continued from Apple Watch”. The other way, the watch gets your newest place from any device. Reading and listening on the watch count toward your streak and goal, and show in Reading History as “On Apple Watch”. All this travels whenever your watch is near your iPhone.

Apple TV

Apple TV gets your library from iCloud, so it needs to be signed in to the same Apple Account, with Sync with iCloud on in Libretto on your iPhone or iPad. Apple TV can’t open ebooks itself, so your iPhone or iPad prepares each book’s text for it and puts it in iCloud, by itself; audiobooks go when you ask for one on the TV. The TV opens at your newest place, and its place, reading time and the chapters it’s read go back to your other devices.

More in iPad, Duo, Watch and TV.

How long it takes, and when something hasn’t arrived

Small things go quickly. While you read or listen, your place goes up about every 30 seconds, and straight away when you close the book, pause, or leave Libretto. Highlights, notes and other changes go as you make them. Your other devices usually hear about it within moments. And whenever Libretto opens or comes back to the front, it checks iCloud itself, so nothing waits long even if a notice goes astray. Book files take as long as their size and your connection need.

If something hasn’t arrived

  1. Look at Settings → iCloud & Storage on both devices. The line under Sync with iCloud says if a device is offline, isn’t signed in, or is out of iCloud storage.

  2. Check both are signed in to the same Apple Account, with iCloud on for Libretto (in the Settings app, tap your name, then iCloud).

  3. Open Libretto on the device where you read or made the change, so it can send anything it hadn’t yet.

  4. On the device that’s missing it, switch away from Libretto and back. It fetches whatever it missed.

If it’s a book that’s missing:

  • Make sure it’s the same file on both. A different edition, or the same title from another store, is a different book to Libretto.
  • A book won’t download? iCloud Drive needs to be on for Libretto on both devices, and Store Books in iCloud on the device that has it.

If your Hardcover sign-in hasn’t reached another device, check that iCloud Keychain is on for both (in the Settings app, under your name → iCloud). Or just connect again in Settings → Hardcover.

Turning sync off

Turn off Sync with iCloud and this device carries on by itself:

  • Everything on it stays: books, places, highlights, notes, your reading log and recaps.
  • Nothing more is sent or fetched. Books that are only on your other devices leave this device’s tabs.
  • What’s already in iCloud stays there, for your other devices.
  • Your Hardcover sign-in stays, as this device’s own.

Turn it back on and Libretto catches up both ways, including whatever you changed while it was off.

Signing in with a different Apple Account

Libretto forgets what came from the old account (books on its other devices, their places and reading days) and starts syncing with the new one. Your own books and reading on this device stay.

Deleting what’s in iCloud

Turning sync off doesn’t delete anything from iCloud. To delete Libretto’s data there, open the Settings app, tap your name, then iCloud, and choose Libretto from what’s using your storage. To take just one device’s reading out, use Remove under Other Devices instead (see Every option on iCloud & Storage).

Privacy

Privacy Everything Libretto syncs lives in your own iCloud account, in a private database that only devices signed in to it can read. There’s no Libretto account or server, and Libretto’s developer has no way to see any of it. The words themselves (book titles, highlights, notes and your devices’ names) go in encrypted fields.

It counts toward your iCloud storage. The library’s details are small, a few megabytes even for a big, well-used library; the books are what take room, and only if you keep them in iCloud. Apple stores it all under its own privacy policy. Libretto’s Privacy Policy has the full list.

Questions

Do I need to set anything up?

No. If your devices are signed in to the same Apple Account with iCloud on, Libretto syncs from the first time you open it. The only extra is iCloud Drive, if you want your book files in iCloud too.

Why did my book open on a different page?

You read further somewhere else more recently, so Libretto took you there. Tap the “Continued from…” pill at the bottom of the page to go back to where this device was.

I have the same book from two places, and they don’t share a place. Why?

Libretto goes by the file, not the title, because two editions don’t line up word for word. Use the same file on every device (Store Books in iCloud makes that easy) and everything lines up.

Will it fill my iCloud storage?

The sync itself takes very little: a few megabytes, mostly recaps and Read-Along timings. Books take more, audiobooks especially. If space is tight, turn off Store Books in iCloud: your place, highlights and everything else still sync.

Do my page themes and fonts sync?

No. Appearance, listening and hands-free settings stay with each device, so each one can be set up for how you use it.

Can my family share my library?

Not through sync: it’s for one Apple Account. Each person gets their own library and reading by signing in with their own.

Does it sync while Libretto is closed?

Your devices can hear about changes in the background, when iOS allows. A device sends its own changes while Libretto is open and as you leave it, and every time Libretto opens it checks for anything it missed.

I gave away my old iPad. How do I stop its reading counting?

In Settings → iCloud & Storage, find it under Other Devices and tap Remove. Its reading days, sessions, finished books and places come out of iCloud and your stats. Your books, highlights and notes stay.

Do I need sync for Apple Watch?

No. The watch works with your iPhone directly. With sync on, the watch’s place and reading time also reach your iPad and your other devices. Apple TV does need sync, since it gets your library through iCloud.
